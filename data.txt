[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive lays down rules concerning the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering and terrorist financing. It establishes obligations for credit institutions, financial institutions, and certain natural and legal persons engaged in activities particularly susceptible to money laundering or terrorist financing.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 2 - Definitions
For the purposes of this Directive, the following definitions apply: Money laundering means any of the conduct set out in Article 1 of Directive 2005/60/EC. Terrorist financing means the provision or collection of funds, by any means, with the intention that they be used or in the knowledge that they are to be used to carry out a terrorist offense.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 5 - Customer Due Diligence
Member States shall ensure that obliged entities apply customer due diligence measures. Customer due diligence shall consist of: identification of the customer and verification of the customer's identity on the basis of documents, data or information obtained from a reliable and independent source.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 6 - Enhanced Due Diligence
Member States shall ensure that obliged entities apply enhanced due diligence measures in higher-risk situations. Enhanced due diligence shall include at least the following measures: conducting enhanced identification and verification of customers, determining the purpose and nature of business relationships, conducting enhanced ongoing monitoring of the business relationship.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 7 - Simplified Due Diligence
Member States may permit obliged entities to apply simplified measures where the risk of money laundering or terrorist financing is low. Member States shall lay down conditions for the application of simplified due diligence based on country risk, product risk, customer risk, and transaction risk.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 8 - Beneficial Ownership
Obliged entities shall take reasonable measures to identify and verify the beneficial owner. For customers that are legal entities, obliged entities shall understand the ownership and control structure of the customer. Member States shall ensure that their registers of beneficial owners contain adequate details of the beneficial ownership of legal entities.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 10 - Ongoing Monitoring
Member States shall ensure that obliged entities apply ongoing monitoring to their business relationships. Such monitoring shall comprise scrutiny of transactions undertaken throughout the course of the business relationship to ensure that they are consistent with the obliged entity's knowledge of the customer, the customer's business and risk profile.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 12 - Politically Exposed Persons
Member States shall ensure that obliged entities apply enhanced due diligence to customers who are, or have been, entrusted with prominent public functions, as well as to their family members and closely associated persons. Obliged entities shall apply measures to determine whether a customer is a politically exposed person.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 13 - Third-Country Transfers
Member States shall ensure that obliged entities do not transfer funds unless they ensure that adequate sanctions screening and beneficial ownership information is available to the Fund Transfer Regulation. Member States shall take measures to ensure that fund transfers contain complete originator and beneficiary information.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 14 - Record Keeping
Member States shall ensure that obliged entities keep records on the facts, evidence and supporting documentation obtained through customer due diligence, for a period of at least five years. Member States shall take appropriate measures to ensure that financial intelligence units have access to these records.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 15 - Suspicious Transaction Reporting
Member States shall ensure that obliged entities report suspicious transactions to the competent FIU without delay and in the manner prescribed by national law. Failure to report suspicious activity constitutes a breach of the Directive's requirements. Reports shall include full details of the suspected activity and relevant customer information.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 16 - Sanctions and Compliance
Member States shall lay down rules on penalties applicable to breaches of the national provisions adopted pursuant to this Directive. Penalties shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. Member States shall inform the Commission of these penalties and any subsequent amendments thereto.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 17 - Competent Authorities
Member States shall designate the competent authorities responsible for the implementation and application of the measures necessary to comply with this Directive. Member States shall ensure appropriate cooperation between competent authorities for the purposes of this Directive, including the exchange of information.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 18 - Financial Intelligence Units
Member States shall ensure that their FIU is independent and autonomous in the execution of its functions. The FIU shall be able to cooperate with other FIUs, EUROPOL, and international partners. FIUs shall have direct access to financial, administrative and criminal records.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 19 - High-Risk Third Countries
The Commission shall establish a list of third countries identified as having strategic AML/CTF deficiencies. Member States shall apply stricter customer due diligence for customers and financial institutions from these high-risk third countries. Enhanced monitoring and reporting requirements apply for transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 20 - Obliged Entities
Obliged entities include: credit institutions, financial institutions, auditors, external accountants and tax advisors, appraisers, real estate agents, casinos, providers of gambling services, and traders in goods dealing in cash. Member States may extend the scope to include other professions and entities based on risk assessment.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 21 - Powers of Member States
Member States shall ensure that competent authorities have the power to obtain any information relevant to the performance of their functions. Competent authorities shall have power to cooperate with each other across borders. Member States shall ensure that obliged entities cooperate fully with competent authorities in the investigation of suspected money laundering.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 22 - Exemptions and Applications
Member States may exempt certain obliged entities from the requirements of this Directive based on risk assessment. Exemptions shall not apply to those engaged in higher-risk activities. Exemptions must be justified by objective grounds related to anti-money laundering effectiveness.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 23 - Sanctions Screening
Obliged entities shall screen customers and beneficiaries against applicable EU and UN sanctions lists. Screening shall take place before establishing business relationships and on an ongoing basis. Member States shall ensure that sanctions violations are reported to relevant authorities without delay.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/849 | Article 24 - Terrorism Financing Prevention
Member States shall take measures to prevent the use of their financial systems for terrorist financing. These measures shall include the identification and tracing of terrorist assets, the application of sanctions, and the reporting of suspected terrorist financing to competent authorities. Member States shall cooperate with the EU and international partners on terrorism financing prevention.

[DOC] Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Article 1 - Subject Matter and Objectives
This Regulation lays down rules relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data. This Regulation protects fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons, in particular their right to the protection of personal data. The rules must take account of the recognition of the right to the protection of personal data.

[DOC] Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Article 5 - Principles Relating to Processing of Personal Data
Personal data shall be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently in relation to the data subject. Personal data shall be collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes. Personal data shall be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed. The controller shall be responsible for implementing data protection by design and by default measures taken at any stage.

[DOC] Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Article 6 - Lawfulness of Processing
Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies: the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes; processing is necessary for the performance of a contract; processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation; processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person; processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest; processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party.

[DOC] Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Article 13 - Information to be Provided Where Personal Data are Collected from the Data Subject
Where personal data relating to a data subject are collected from the data subject, the controller shall provide the data subject with at least the following information: the identity and the contact details of the controller; the purposes of the processing for which the personal data are intended as well as the legal basis for the processing; the recipients or categories of recipients of the personal data, if any; information about the right to request access to personal data; information about the right to rectification or erasure.

[DOC] Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Article 32 - Security of Processing
Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation and the nature, scope, context and purposes of processing as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity, the controller and processor shall implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including inter alia as appropriate encryption and the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience of processing systems and services.

[DOC] Directive (EU) 2015/2366 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive establishes rules on the provision of payment services in the internal market. It aims to create a harmonised framework for payment services in order to facilitate the provision of cross-border payment services and to ensure consumer protection and the safety of payments.

[DOC] Directive (EU) 2015/2366 | Article 4 - Definitions
Payment service provider means a natural or legal person providing payment services. Payment account means an account held in the name of one or more payment service users which is used for the execution of payment transactions. Payment transaction means an action initiated by a payer or on the payer's behalf or by a payee to place funds at the disposal of a payee or to instruct a payment service provider to place funds at the disposal of a payee.

[DOC] Directive (EU) 2015/2366 | Article 5 - Authorisation of Payment Service Providers
Payment service providers shall be authorised by the competent authorities of the Member State in which they are established. Competent authorities shall only authorise payment service providers that satisfy the requirements laid down in this Directive and in the implementing technical standards. Payment service providers shall at all times maintain own funds and mechanisms for safeguarding funds to protect against risks.

[DOC] Directive (EU) 2015/2366 | Article 38 - Strong Customer Authentication
Payment service providers shall require strong customer authentication when a payer accesses its payment account online, when a payer initiates an electronic payment transaction, or when a payer carries out any action through a remote channel which may imply a risk of fraud or unauthorised payment.

[DOC] Directive (EU) 2015/2366 | Article 62 - Liability for Unauthorised Payment Transactions
A payer shall be liable for unauthorised payment transactions up to a maximum of fifty euros. The payer shall bear the losses resulting from unauthorised payment transactions if the payer has, with intent or gross negligence, failed to keep safe the personalised security credentials or notify the payment service provider without undue delay.

[DOC] Directive 2014/65/EU | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive establishes rules on the provision of investment services and regulated activities on financial instruments in the internal market. It lays down rules concerning the prudential supervision of investment firms and the organisation of markets in financial instruments.

[DOC] Directive 2014/65/EU | Article 2 - Definitions
An investment firm means any natural or legal person whose occupation or business is the provision of investment services and/or the performance of investment activities on a professional basis. A financial instrument means any contract that creates a financial asset for one party and a financial liability or equity instrument for the other party.

[DOC] Directive 2014/65/EU | Article 16 - General Principles
Investment firms shall act honestly, fairly and professionally in accordance with the best interests of their clients. Investment firms shall manage conflicts of interest in a way that ensures that the fair treatment of clients is not compromised. Investment firms shall be required to ensure that relevant persons obtain and maintain the knowledge and competence required to support the client in understanding the nature and risks of investment services and financial instruments.

[DOC] Directive 2014/65/EU | Article 25 - Suitability
Before providing personalised recommendations or discretionary portfolio management services, investment firms shall obtain the necessary information regarding the client's knowledge, experience, financial situation and investment objectives in order to make a proper assessment of the suitability of any advice or submission of recommendations that is to be provided.

[DOC] Directive 2014/65/EU | Article 37 - Inducements
Investment firms may pay or be paid inducements from third parties which do not contravene the obligation to act in accordance with the best interests of the client. Any such payments shall be made transparent to the client and shall be designed to enhance the quality of the relevant service to the client.

[DOC] EU Directive 2013/36/EU | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive establishes prudential requirements for credit institutions and investment firms regarding own funds, governance structures, remuneration policies, and liquidity. It creates a harmonized regulatory framework for the prudential supervision of credit institutions across the EU and establishes the European Banking Authority framework.

[DOC] EU Directive 2013/36/EU | Article 23 - Composition of Own Funds
Credit institutions shall have Own Funds composed of Tier 1 capital and Tier 2 capital. Tier 1 capital comprises Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) and Additional Tier 1 (AT1) capital. Tier 2 capital shall comprise capital instruments and subordinated claims meeting the regulatory requirements and instruments included in an institution's capital buffer.

[DOC] EU Directive 2013/36/EU | Article 74 - Internal Governance
Credit institutions shall have robust governance arrangements, including a clear organisational structure with effective and prudent management. The management body shall have overall responsibility for the governance of the institution. Remuneration policies shall be consistent with and promote sound and effective risk management and shall not encourage excessive risk-taking.

[DOC] EU Directive 2013/36/EU | Article 95 - Liquidity Requirements
Member States shall ensure that credit institutions maintain adequate liquidity buffers to ensure they can meet their payment obligations. Credit institutions shall calculate their liquidity coverage ratio taking into account the stressed scenario set out in the regulatory technical standards. Member States shall not impose liquidity requirements that are stricter than those specified in Union law.

[DOC] EU Regulation 575/2013 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Regulation establishes uniform prudential rules applicable to credit institutions and investment firms throughout the Union. The Regulation specifies minimum capital requirements to ensure the safety and soundness of credit institutions and investment firms. It establishes requirements for own funds, capital buffers, and leverage ratios.

[DOC] EU Regulation 575/2013 | Article 72 - Common Equity Tier 1 Items
Common Equity Tier 1 items comprise instruments and reserves that meet the following conditions: they are issued directly by the institution, they represent the most subordinated claim on the assets or income of the institution, and they provide full flexibility in the distribution of earnings and capital repayment. Common Equity Tier 1 items shall not include hybrid instruments or debt securities.

[DOC] EU Regulation 575/2013 | Article 425 - Operational Risk
Credit institutions shall calculate the capital requirement for operational risk using an Approach chosen by the competent authorities. The standardized approach allocates capital requirements based on the institution's gross income in specific business lines. The advanced measurement approaches shall be based on the institution's own internal estimates of operational risk losses.

[DOC] EU Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Regulation establishes transparency requirements for the financial market participants and financial advisors regarding the integration of sustainability risks and the promotion of environmental and social characteristics or sustainable investment objectives. It aims to improve transparency of financial products regarding environmental, social and governance (ESG) factors and to prevent greenwashing.

[DOC] EU Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 | Article 4 - Transparency of the Integration of Sustainability Risks
Financial market participants shall make public how they integrate sustainability risks in their investment decision-making process. Financial market participants shall disclose how they ensure that the persons involved in the investment decision-making process are not prevented from properly integrating sustainability considerations. Financial advisors shall inform clients about how sustainability risks are integrated when providing advice.

[DOC] EU Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 | Article 8 - Promotion of Environmental or Social Characteristics
Financial market participants shall make clear and accessible information available on how a financial product promotes environmental or social characteristics. The information shall describe how the financial product will be invested to meet the promoted characteristics and shall indicate whether investments are aligned with sustainable development objectives.

[DOC] EU Directive 2014/59/EU | Article 1 - Objectives
This Directive creates a common framework for the resolution of failing credit institutions and investment firms in the Union. The objectives of resolution are to maintain financial stability and continuity of critical functions, to minimize public expenses on banking bailouts, and to protect depositors and investors through deposit guarantee and investor compensation schemes.

[DOC] EU Directive 2014/59/EU | Article 21 - Resolution Principles
Resolution authorities shall apply resolution tools and powers with a view to achieving the resolution objectives. The resolution action shall be taken as soon as it becomes apparent that an institution is likely to fail. Resolution authorities shall apply the write-down of capital instruments and bail-in of unsecured liabilities of the institution.

[DOC] EU Directive 2014/59/EU | Article 36 - Bail-In Powers
Resolution authorities shall have the power to reduce the amount owed to creditors by write-down or conversion of liabilities of a failing institution. The bail-in shall be applied in accordance with the creditor hierarchy except for certain protected liabilities such as covered deposits and privileged deposits.

[DOC] EU Regulation 1093/2010 | Article 1 - Establishment of the European Banking Authority
The European Banking Authority is established as an independent authority with legal personality. The EBA shall contribute to ensuring consistent and effective prudential regulation of credit institutions and investment firms and consumer protection in the Union. The EBA shall promote supervisory convergence.

[DOC] EU Regulation 1093/2010 | Article 8 - Tasks of the Authority
The EBA shall develop draft regulatory technical standards and implementing technical standards to ensure consistent prudential regulation. The EBA shall issue guidelines and recommendations to ensure consistent application of Union law. The EBA shall cooperate with national competent authorities to ensure proper coordination of resolution processes.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/2366 | Article 67 - Refund of Unauthorised Electronic Payment Transactions
A payment service user shall have the right to a refund of an unauthorised electronic payment transaction from the payment service provider. However, the user shall not be entitled to a refund if they acted with gross negligence in failing to keep safe the personalised security credentials or failing to notify of loss or theft.

[DOC] EU Directive 2015/2366 | Article 98 - Market Conduct
Payment service providers shall treat customers fairly and shall not engage in unfair commercial practices or misleading advertising. Payment service providers shall not place obligations on payment service users which exceed those agreed in the relevant contract or which are stricter than those imposed by EU law.

[DOC] EU Directive 2009/65/EC | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive establishes rules for the authorization and ongoing regulation of Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities (UCITS) in order to facilitate the management of collective investment portfolios. The Directive aims to create a single rulebook for UCITS to enable free movement of capital within the EU.

[DOC] EU Directive 2009/65/EC | Article 12 - Requirement to Hold Authorisation
An undertaking shall not be managed or marketed as a UCITS unless it holds authorisation from the competent authority as a UCITS. Authorisation shall be granted only if the undertaking meets all the requirements of this Directive. Competent authorities shall refuse authorisation if the applicant fails to satisfy the required conditions.

[DOC] EU Directive 2014/91/EU | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive amends Directive 2009/65/EC to establish rules on the authorization and regulation of European long-term investment funds and funds of funds. It facilitates the cross-border distribution and management of collective investments throughout the Union by harmonizing requirements for fund managers and depositaries.

[DOC] EU Regulation 236/2012 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Regulation establishes a common regulatory framework for short selling and credit default swaps in the Union in order to ensure the smooth operation of financial markets and to avoid risks to financial stability. The Regulation requires notification and disclosure of net short positions and imposes restrictions on naked short selling. 

[DOC] EU Regulation 236/2012 | Article 5 - Emergency powers of National Competent Authorities
Member States' competent authorities shall have powers to impose restrictions on short selling if there is a significant fall in the price of a financial instrument or in the prices of financial instruments in a given market. Such restrictions shall be temporary and proportionate and shall not duplicate restrictions imposed by ESMA.

[DOC] Basel III Framework - Liquidity Coverage Ratio
The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) requires banks to hold an adequate amount of high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) that can be easily and immediately converted into cash to meet their liquidity needs over a 30-calendar-day stress scenario. Banks must maintain a minimum LCR of 100 percent. HQLA comprises level 1 assets and level 2 assets which face certain haircuts.

[DOC] EU Regulation 2023/1114 | Article 1 - Subject Matter (MiCA)
This Regulation establishes a harmonized regulatory framework for marketing and custody of crypto-assets in the Union. It aims to ensure consumer protection and financial stability while promoting innovation in the crypto-asset market. Member States shall establish competencies to authorize and supervise crypto-asset service providers including exchanges, wallets, and lending platforms.

[DOC] EU Regulation 2023/1114 | Article 15 - Authorization of Crypto-Asset Service Providers
Crypto-asset exchange service providers that market crypto-assets in the Union shall be authorized by the competent authorities of the Member State in which they are established. Authorization can only be granted to legal persons established in the Union. Crypto-asset exchange service providers shall comply with requirements regarding governance, risk management, and operational resilience.

[DOC] EU Regulation 2023/1114 | Article 53 - Customer Due Diligence for Crypto-Assets
Crypto-asset service providers shall apply customer due diligence measures to all customers and beneficial owners. Due diligence shall include identification of the customer, verification of the customer's identity, and understanding the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship. Enhanced due diligence applies for high-risk customers and jurisdictions.

[DOC] EU Directive 2026/2408 | Article 1 - Subject Matter (DORA)
This Directive establishes a framework for digital operational resilience of financial entities. It aims to ensure high levels of cybersecurity and operational resilience in light of increased digital threats. Financial entities shall implement technical, organizational and governance measures to ensure continuity and protection of critical functions in case of digital incidents.

[DOC] EU Directive 2026/2408 | Article 20 - Incident Reporting
Financial entities shall report major operational or security incidents to competent authorities without undue delay and no later than 72 hours. The report shall include details of the incident, impact assessment, and mitigation measures undertaken. Member States shall establish centralized incident reporting frameworks and share information with competent authorities and sectoral bodies.

[DOC] EU Regulation 2024/517 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Regulation amends Regulation (EU) No 648/2012 on over-the-counter derivatives, central counterparties and trade repositories to enhance oversight of non-EU counterparties and improve transparency of derivative transactions. Enhanced reporting requirements apply to all derivatives transactions regardless of counterparty location or trade execution venue.

[DOC] EU Directive 2024/1139 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive establishes a framework for the transfer of venture capital and private equity funds in the internal market. It creates harmonized due diligence standards for fund transfers and requires Member States to designate competent authorities responsible for the authorization and supervision of fund transfers to ensure investor protection and market integrity.

[DOC] EU Regulation 2024/886 | Article 1 - Subject Matter (Net Zero Alignment)
This Regulation establishes requirements for financial market participants regarding the alignment of financial products with net-zero objectives. Market participants shall make available information on how their financial products contribute to or do not contribute to the transition to a net-zero economy. Greenwashing prevention measures include verification of sustainability claims and alignment with EU taxonomy standards.

[DOC] EU Regulation 2024/886 | Article 14 - Information Disclosure for Net-Zero Products
Financial market participants offering net-zero aligned products shall provide prospective clients with clear information about the product's alignment with net-zero objectives, transition timeline, and impact measurement. Information shall include assumption and limitations of net-zero alignment strategies. Misleading or unsupported net-zero claims are prohibited and may result in administrative penalties.

[DOC] EU Directive (EU) 2025/456 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Directive establishes enhanced cybersecurity requirements for critical infrastructure in the financial sector. Financial institutions classified as critical operators shall maintain advanced detection and prevention systems for cyber-attacks and establish incident response plans approved by competent authorities. Cross-border cooperation mechanisms for threat intelligence sharing are mandatory.

[DOC] EU Regulation 2025/712 | Article 1 - Subject Matter
This Regulation establishes a framework for sustainable finance taxonomy updates and expands the definition of environmentally sustainable economic activities to include transitions in emerging sectors. Updated taxonomy standards apply to renewable energy, energy efficiency retrofitting, and circular economy initiatives. Member States shall review classification and reporting requirements annually.
